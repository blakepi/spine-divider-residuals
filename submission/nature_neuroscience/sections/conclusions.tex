\section{Conclusion}

The load-normalized spine-neck ratio is a compact coordinate for the classical local voltage divider. Its most defensible use is to state the expected low-frequency head-to-dendrite transfer, $\Gamma_{\mathrm{div}}=1/(1+\SMI)$, and then measure the residual from that expectation. In SPINE, those residuals are the main scientific object: they show when transient conductance dynamics, impedance, synaptic drive, active state, morphology, downstream filtering, and measurement uncertainty make the scalar descriptor incomplete. The practical rule is therefore simple: use the ratio for first-pass local divider reasoning, and use residuals plus richer electrical descriptors for peak transfer, head amplitude, somatic impact, nonlinear response, thresholded classes, and equivalence claims.
