2 Numerical Verication and Benchmarks
The passive baseline was checked against geometry identities, synaptic normalization, current
signs, matrix limits, timestep renement, high-synapse stability, and Backward Euler versus
Crank-Nicolson agreement. The baseline verication script generated the passive source data
and classied all representative reference target values as passing workow-specied target-case
tolerances. The most stringent target-case tolerance class means absolute error ≤ 0.005 or relative
error ≤ 2%, not bitwise equality.
    An independent direct-matrix benchmark was added for the passive three-compartment baseline
reference cases. The benchmark independently parsed the baseline parameters, recomputed capac-
itance/leak/neck terms and the double-exponential synaptic conductance, assembled the passive
matrices, and solved the Backward Euler system without calling the production passive simula-
tion function as its benchmark engine. Compared with existing SPINE trace CSVs, the maximum
all-trace absolute dierence was 1.25 × 10−13 mV, the maximum head-amplitude dierence was
1.42 × 10−14 mV, and the maximum Γh→d dierence was 7.55 × 10−15 .
    DC analytic limits were also checked. The spine-omitted two-node load formula gave Rin,d =
144.48669201520912 MΩ, and the direct DC solve gave 144.48669201520914 MΩ. Divider predictions
for the low, intermediate, and high reference cases were 0.9912648, 0.8970688, and 0.4306959. The
zero-neck and very-large-neck limiting cases approached local transfer values of 1 and 0, respectively.
    The BE-versus-CN reference-case comparison remains an internal scheme-consistency check.
Across the three baseline reference cases, the maximum peak dierences were 0.00203 mV for Ah ,
0.000534 mV for Ad , 0.000189 mV for As , 0.000500 for Γh→d , and 0.000180 for Γh→s . The maximum
trace voltage dierence was 0.112 mV in the high-ρL case.
    Passive morphology verication included sparse versus dense equivalence, spatial convergence,
sinusoidal impedance checks, logarithmic-chirp impedance checks, and distributed-neck compar-
isons. Sinusoidal checks produced maximum relative amplitude error 0.0054. Chirp checks produced
maximum relative amplitude error 0.072. Spatial convergence passed at the 16-versus-32 segment
comparison with relative Γh→s dierence 0.014.
    Active-mechanism verication included AMPA peak normalization, NMDA magnesium-block
monotonicity, NMDA current-voltage relief, active-current sign checks, gate bounds, positive gate
time constants, resting-state stability, timestep renement, limiting-case solver comparison, strong-
drive stability, and active unit checks. All 12 active verication rows passed.
    NEURON was not importable in the project runtime and no NEURON result is claimed. Brian2
was installed only in a project-local optional validation environment and used for Brian2-hosted
scoped checks of the three passive baseline cases, one cylindrical distributed-neck passive case, and
one AMPA/NMDA synapse-only case. These checks used Brian2 as a unit, clock, and state host
with an independent SI backward-Euler update. They passed predened tolerances and provide
representative implementation-check support, but they do not validate all rows, restrained active
channels, or biological calibration. Brian2 is not a required SPINE runtime dependency.

3 Release, Environment, and Residual-Control Provenance
Residual timing controls, row provenance, validation coverage, high-ρL stress-test summaries, and
environment metadata are reported once in the tables at the end of this supplement. The residual-
control table reports the manuscript's separate-peak metric alongside matched-head-peak and area-
under-curve controls, because the DC divider is a low-frequency reference and the simulated traces
are transient conductance-driven responses.
    The reconciled row-provenance table is the authoritative denominator accounting. It reports
3,718 included deterministic design-derived residual rows, with 3,584 non-exploratory rows and 134
exploratory stress-test rows. The original N=96 uncertainty screen is retained only as archived
provenance when listed separately; it has denominator contribution 0 in that listing because adding
it again would create the erroneous 3,910-row total.
    The environment table separates the default project/document runtime from the optional Brian2
validation environment. Package availability therefore should be read by context, not as a statement
that every dependency was importable in every runtime or required by the SPINE solver.


4 Uncertainty, Identiability, and Predictor Stability
The uncertainty analysis used deterministic Latin-hypercube ensembles and deterministic resam-
pling diagnostics. A robustness audit extended this to progressive N=96, 192, 384, and 768 ensem-
bles. The global uncertainty seed was 202605; progressive-sampling ensembles used deterministic
osets 202605 + 5100 + N . The original 48-versus-96 ρL convergence failure was preserved and
interpreted as a small-sample warning in a skewed ρL distribution. By N=768, nal ρL median con-
vergence passed with relative change 0.012, and nal all-median convergence passed with maximum
relative change 0.029.
    Radius uncertainty was analyzed independently because of the quadratic radius term in Rneck .
The radius audit used 25 deterministic osets spanning ±2 SD, with SD = 0.018 µm and clipping to
0.060.22 µm. At N=768, 184 of 768 sampled parameter combinations changed ρL class under the
analysis-dened radius-error model, a design fraction of 24.0%. Low-class combinations ipped in
22.9% of cases, all 11 intermediate combinations ipped, and no high-ρL combinations were present.
The median radius-induced ρL fold range was 3.44. These values are deterministic design summaries,
not biological rates.
    High-ρL uncertainty coverage is absent in the main N=768 deterministic ensemble. The ensemble
contains 757 low-ρL combinations, 11 intermediate combinations, and zero high-ρL combinations.
High-ratio behavior in the manuscript is therefore supported by reference, designed challenge cases,
and targeted extension rows rather than by the N=768 uncertainty ensemble.
    Predictor comparisons used Pearson association, Spearman association, deterministic resampling
summaries, cross-validated RMSE, and multivariable diagnostics. Exact top predictor labels were
not fully stable because near-tied dynamic-ratio variants changed order. The manuscript therefore
emphasizes descriptor families and mechanistic interpretation.

5 Active and Nonlinear Mechanism Notes
AMPA and NMDA synapses are opt-in extensions. NMDA includes a voltage-dependent magne-
sium block based on established reduced forms [1, 2]. Na and delayed-rectier K conductances follow
Hodgkin-Huxley-style conductance-current logic [3]. HCN, A-type K, and restrained calcium con-
ductances are included as generic active-state stressors with provenance from dendritic physiology
literature [47].
    These conductances are not cell-type-calibrated densities. They are disabled in the baseline
reference conguration and should not be interpreted as tted biological parameters. Their purpose
in this manuscript is to test whether passive load-ratio conclusions survive nonlinear and active stress
tests. The implementation map below links each mechanism to its parameterization; source-code
functions are listed in the accompanying reproducibility reports and public release manifest.

HCN, potassium, AMPA/NMDA, clustering, synchrony, combined perturbations, and conicting

    The exploratory uncertainty screen uses N=8 sampled combinations per scenario. It is a sen-

scenarios are best cited as examples showing that synaptic strength, active state, clustering, and
timing can alter voltage outcomes without changing morphology-dened ρL .

9 Extended Limitations
SPINE is a transparent computational framework, not a calibrated biological reconstruction. The
baseline reference track preserves pre-specied assumptions even when they are simplied. The
plausibility-revised and active-extension tracks are explicitly separated. Exploratory perturbation
scenarios are supplemental stress tests and are not disease models.

                                                                                                    13
    The analytic divider is a DC small-signal reference. The simulated targets in this manuscript are
transient conductance-based peak responses, so residuals from the divider are expected and infor-
mative. The independent direct-matrix benchmark checks the passive three-compartment baseline
implementation against an independently assembled equation solve, but it does not check every mor-
phology, active, uncertainty, or exploratory output. NEURON cross-checking was not performed.
    The deterministic uncertainty ensembles are sensitivity designs, not experimentally tted pos-
terior distributions. The N=768 ensemble contains no high-ρL rows, so high-isolation uncertainty
remains a targeted limitation. Radius uncertainty, unmodeled ultrastructure, cytoplasmic resistiv-
ity uncertainty, segmentation error, local synaptic receptor composition, and active conductance
distributions can all alter interpretation.
    The current package does not include posterior inference from experimental voltage traces, cell-
type-specic channel tting, full morphology libraries, biochemical calcium reaction-diusion, re-
ceptor tracking, signaling cascades, or receptor-level plasticity mechanisms. These are future
validation targets, not hidden assumptions of the current manuscript.
